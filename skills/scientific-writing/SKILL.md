# Scientific Writing

Write for the intended discipline, venue, and reader. Use IMRAD or the structure appropriate to the paper type, but do not impose a template when the user or venue specifies another structure.

## Workflow

1. Identify the research question, audience, document type, target venue if known, available sources, data, and required sections.
2. Separate source facts, project results, derivations, interpretations, assumptions, and proposed future work.
3. Draft a compact section outline, then write coherent prose. Use full paragraphs for manuscript prose; bullets and tables are appropriate in plans, checklists, or technical notes.
4. Add figures, tables, graphical abstracts, reporting checklists, and citation styles only when requested by the venue, user, or communication need.
5. Check claims against sources, methods against the reported data, symbols and units across sections, and references against the actual cited work.

## Evidence and limits

Keep assumptions, sample definitions, uncertainty, error, model conditions, negative results, failed experiments, and applicability limits where they affect interpretation. Do not use positive wording to conceal an unsupported result. Do not claim peer review, compliance, reproducibility, or validation beyond the work actually completed.

## Figures and tables

Use `publication-figures` for publication-quality plots and the relevant format skill for PDF, DOCX, PPTX, or XLSX. Do not generate AI figures merely because a manuscript is being written. Empirical figures must come from the data and preserve meaningful transformations; schematics are optional and labeled as conceptual.

## Formal output

Deliver only the requested manuscript, source, figures, tables, and necessary reproducibility files. Keep tool logs, agent roles, progress notes, AI identity statements, internal audit language, and generic disclaimers outside the formal document. Follow the target journal's official guidance when the venue is known.
