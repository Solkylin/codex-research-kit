# Scientific Critical Thinking

State the claim, evidence, assumptions, comparison, and decision that the analysis must support. Distinguish factual support, causal inference, extrapolation, speculation, and missing evidence.

Check the failure modes that matter for the task: selection and measurement bias, confounding, leakage, sample size and power, dependence, missing data, multiple comparisons, model misspecification, uncertainty, reproducibility, and external validity. For engineering work also check units, coordinate conventions, sampling, delays, constraints, and operating range.

Use figures, tables, diagrams, or formal grading frameworks only when they improve the requested analysis or are required by a venue. Preserve negative findings and unresolved uncertainty in the result. Keep process notes and internal review language outside the formal deliverable.
